Question No:14

An optically active alkyl halide 'X' undergoes hydrolysis to form an alcohol. The alcohol is found to be optically inactive. The most probable reason is that:

1 ) The reaction proceeds by an SN2 mechanism with complete inversion. 2 ) The reaction proceeds through a planar carbocation, giving equal amounts of two enantiomers.
3 ) The product formed is a meso compound. 4 ) The alcohol is always optically inactive.

Your Option: 2

Correct Option: 2

Score:4/-1 1.00

Detailed Answer: If an optically active alkyl halide gives an optically inactive alcohol, the most likely reason is racemization through an SN1 mechanism.
The planar carbocation intermediate is attacked from both sides, producing equal amounts of two enantiomers whose optical rotations cancel each other.
SN2 would produce only one enantiomer with inverted configuration, which would still be optically active.
The product is not necessarily a meso compound, and alcohols can certainly be optically active if they contain a chiral center.
