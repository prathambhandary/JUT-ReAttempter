Your Option: 4

Correct Option: 2

Score:4/-1 0.00

Detailed Answer: According to Coulomb's Law, the electrostatic force between two point charges is given by:
  F = k e q 1 q 2 r 2
where: k e is Coulomb's constant, q 1 and q 2 are the magnitudes of the charges,   r is the distance between them.
For two point charges A and B , the forces on each charge due to the other are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction.

Question No:18

The angle between the particle velocity and wave velocity in a transverse wave is (except when the particle passes through the mean position)

1 ) π radian 2 ) π 2 radian
3 ) Zero radian 4 ) π 4 radian

Your Option: 2

Correct Option: 2

Score:4/-1 1.00

Detailed Answer: In a transverse wave, the particle velocity is always perpendicular to the displacement of the particle, and the wave velocity is along the direction of propagation of the wave.
The particle velocity is the velocity at which each particle of the medium moves as the wave passes. This velocity is tangential to the particle's motion.
The wave velocity, on the other hand, is the velocity at which the wave moves through the medium, propagating from one point to another.
At any point except when the particle passes through the mean position (where the particle is momentarily stationary), the angle between the particle velocity and the wave velocity is   π 2 radians. This is because the particle moves perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
Thus, the angle between the particle velocity and wave velocity is   π 2 radian.

Correct Option: 4

Score:4/-1 1.00

Detailed Answer: Step 1: Recall the typical stress-strain curve
A typical stress-strain curve for a ductile material (like mild steel) has the following regions:
O to B - Elastic region
Stress is proportional to strain (Hooke's law)
Material returns to original shape when stress is removed
2. B to C -Yielding region (also called plastic region begins)
Yield point at B
Strain increases rapidly with almost constant stress
3. C to D - Strain hardening region
Stress increases again after yielding
Material can take more stress before ultimate failure
4. D to E - Necking and fracture
Stress decreases
Material ultimately breaks
Step 2: Identify region B to D
B to C: Plastic deformation / yielding
Strain increases rapidly, but stress is almost constant C to D : Strain hardening
Stress increases again, but strain still increases
So, overall, in B to D, the material is plastically deforming:Strain increases rapidly even for a small change in stress
Step 3: Key idea
Elastic region: small strain for small stress (linear)
Plastic region (B-D): large strain for small stress changes
Necking (D-E): material approaches fracture
Answer: Strain increases rapidly even for a small change
